Concept sheet · 06
What it takes to build one
Three honest tiers — none of them cheap, none of them science fiction
DIY / Maker
weekend project, one person
Advanced Prototype
makerspace + small team
Near-Production
contract EE/ME + textile partner
Core sensing
ESP32 + BNO055 IMU breakout
nRF52840 + BNO086 IMU, compact mmWave/Doppler module for proximity
Redundant IMUs, integrated mmWave + ToF array, dedicated fusion coprocessor
Haptics ×4
Coin ERM motors — ~60–100 ms rise time
LRA + DRV2605L haptic driver — ~5–15 ms rise time
Closed-loop LRA array, auto-tunes resonance to skin contact
Signal path
Wired I²C, breadboard harness
Thin wired harness or BLE 5.2 (wired is lower-latency and the safer default)
Low-latency wired backbone; wireless reserved for a companion app only
Power
500 mAh LiPo + TP4056 charger
2000 mAh LiPo, USB-C, ~4–8 h runtime
Distributed cells for balance, hot-swap, wireless charging dock
Emblems / shell
3D-printed housings + WS2812 LED strip
Flex-PCB + diffuser lens, silicone-overmolded housing
Medical-grade silicone, laser-welded seams, no stitching to fail
Base layer
Sewn onto an off-the-shelf compression layer
Laminated 4-way-stretch fabric, purpose-cut
Body-scanned compression garment, IP54+ rated

The DIY tier proves the concept and gets the timing story right — it will feel laggy (ERM motors alone add 50–100 ms most of the budget). The jump to LRA + a real haptic driver in the Advanced tier is where the "pulse before contact" effect actually starts to feel real.
