Component design · form-factor study
Cue Node — the housing doesn't have to be a kite
Five candidate shapes for the same internal stack, judged on function first
Kite
Strongest brand read, reads as "hero tech" at a glance. Four sharp corners are fatigue points under repeated flex.
KEEP — WAIST HUB ONLY
Disc
No orientation to get wrong, even pressure against skin, drops onto any off-the-shelf round LRA with zero custom tooling.
RECOMMENDED — PALM + SOLE
Hexagon
Tiles edge-to-edge if a future build gangs multiple sensors together. Six flex points is worse fatigue-wise than the disc's zero.
SHELVED
Capsule
Lowest profile, bends beautifully along the palm crease. Has a "right way up" — a real failure mode during a fast glove change.
SHELVED
Teardrop
The point could double as a literal directional cue. A molded point that thin is the first thing that tears on repeated flex.
SHELVED
Recommendation
Split the housing by mechanical role instead of using one shape everywhere. The four flex-critical, skin-contact nodes — both palms, both soles — go to the disc: it's the boring, correct choice where durability and comfort matter more than silhouette. The kite stays exactly where it already earns its keep — the waist core hub, which barely flexes and is the single most visible point on the suit. Same internal stack (diffuser, LED, flex-PCB, actuator) in both housings.
